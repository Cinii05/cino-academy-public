# Reader testing and coherent argument editing

Borrow the **approach**, not source text, from open-source doc-coauthoring workflows: gather minimum context, improve section structure, and test the document as a new reader. This is a review tool, not a substitute for independent human marker feedback.

## Procedure
1. Identify audience, central question/decision, key thesis and accepted source boundary.
2. Predict 3–6 realistic reader questions about unfamiliar terms, missing assumptions, evidence, scope and requested action.
3. In a genuinely fresh context, when a separate model or independent reviewer is available, pass the document and those questions **without private conversation context**. Record what the reader inferred, what could not be found, and passages causing confusion.
4. If no independent context exists, perform an explicitly labelled SELF-REVIEW, not an 'independent reader test'.
5. Map ambiguous or unsupported sections to a smallest necessary change and retest the relevant questions. Do not invent an answer to a missing evidence question.
6. For academic arguments, test each paragraph's job: specific claim -> inspected evidence -> analysis/limitation -> reason the next paragraph follows. This is an editorial aid, not a fixed four-sentence template.

## Review result
Passage/location, reader question, actual answer or failure, importance, proposed text/structural revision and whether the fix depends on missing evidence. Do not claim a First, full rubric alignment, independent marker review or source corroboration merely because the text reads smoothly.

## Absent-document honesty

When no document was supplied, do not imply the assistant authored, edited, read, assessed, or tested it. Avoid generic first-person statements like 'I wrote or edited the report' unless that action actually occurred. Label independent reading, source validity, and report-specific findings `NOT_VERIFIED`; offer a concise protocol and realistic reader questions instead.
