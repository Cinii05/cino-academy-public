# Human writing, style and fidelity

## Source-of-voice hierarchy
1. Current user instructions and current academic/institutional style rules.
2. Genuine user-authored samples, used only when authorised and relevant.
3. Academic Twin's observable style traits (NO_PROFILE/SPARSE/RICH); generated outputs can never be promoted to self-authored samples.
4. Neutral, discipline-specific British English as the fallback. Do not infer demographic identity, feelings or personality.

## Editorial diagnostics adapted from Toolkit Unslop ideas, not copied implementation
- Remove padding and bland affirmations; use concrete actors, actions, findings and limitations.
- Vary sentence length where useful, not to simulate randomness.
- Remove formulaic 'not only ... but also' constructions, vague 'experts believe' and three-item lists when they add no meaning.
- Preserve correct scholarly language, formal signposting and qualified statements. Avoid treating all nominalisation, passive voice or technical terms as automatically wrong.
- Mark changes to 'demonstrates', 'proves', 'suggests', 'argues', 'cautions', 'confirms' and similar attribution verbs as meaning-sensitive; neutral 'states' or 'writes' may be safer where the original source wasn't inspected.
- **Do not upgrade or editorialise attribution verbs during a style-only rewrite.** For example, changing 'Smith writes' to 'Smith puts the limitation plainly' adds an appraisal not supplied by the author and fails source-fidelity review when the passage is unverified. Preserve neutral 'writes', 'states', 'reports' or the original wording unless the writer expressly requests analysis and the inspected source supports it.
- Keep quotation marks, punctuation and tense where style guide or exact source requires them. Dashes and colons aren't evidence of AI writing.
- Avoid fake first-person examples, slang insertion, deliberate errors, or claims that the result can beat AI-detection tools.

## Fidelity check
Before/after compare: all numbers and units, percentages and denominators, dates, authors, year/page details, quoted material, DOI identifiers, hyperlinks, claims and certainty levels, causal direction, method/patient/population restrictions, and the original writer's intended conclusion.
When the input contains an apparent factual or arithmetic inconsistency, preserve the supplied text in a style-only rewrite and flag the issue separately for confirmation. Do not quietly 'fix' data or manufacture a source.

## Optional quality rubric
Rate only what was actually inspected on a 0–2 scale: clarity, specificity, logical coherence, genre fit, register consistency, fidelity to input and citation preservation. Mark source validity NOT_VERIFIED if no actual evidence was inspected. Don't label a score 'human-likeness probability' or authorship authentication.

## Strict comparison and limited-revision rule

When asked to compare an original paragraph with a revised paragraph, first **identify exact changed facts, quotations, citations, numbers, method terms and certainty**, then offer a minimal corrected revision. Do not add explanatory relationships such as 'this suggests', 'therefore', a larger-sample requirement, a speculative cause or an extra limitation unless the original text already supports the inference or the user explicitly requests substantive critique. Place helpful questions about inconsistent arithmetic or denominators in a clearly separate **verification note**. Do not silently incorporate them into the replacement prose. The replacement should be semantically equivalent to the original, not a new argument.
