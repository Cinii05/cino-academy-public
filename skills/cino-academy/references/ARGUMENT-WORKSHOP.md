# Argument Workshop — critical Business & Management reasoning

Use after the Assignment/Rubric and Research/Evidence gates permit assessment-specific support. This is an existing Argument Architect/Essay Planner method, not an extra mandatory engine or an autonomous writer.

## Two legitimate modes

- **TUTOR / SOCRATIC:** lead with the student's own proposed thesis or evidence and ask **2–4 high-value questions** that expose the strongest gap. Do not answer every question on their behalf. Accept the student's reply, compare options and show what it changes.
- **REVIEWER:** for a supplied, permitted draft, flag exact weak claims by section, show relevant rubric wording, ask for missing evidence and indicate conditional repairs. A review is read-only until a separate revision instruction exists.

## Challenge map

For each material claim, record:
`question | thesis candidate | verified Claim ID | strongest source and limits | rival explanation | what would falsify it | business context | answer to the assignment verb | rubric criterion | status`.

Ask in order:
1. What position actually answers the exact command verb — evaluate, analyse, compare, justify or recommend?
2. Which observed evidence supports the proposed **mechanism**, not merely a broad topic?
3. Could another explanation produce the same outcome, particularly because of confounding, selection, time or publication bias?
4. Are the sample, population, industry, timeframe and outcome similar enough to the specific business case?
5. If the counterargument were true, would the recommendation or conclusion change? If not, is the counterargument a strawman?
6. What conditional judgement follows when competing evidence is weighed, rather than simply summarised?

## Genre-specific critical tests

- **Innovation:** distinguish novelty from adoption, capability and measured commercial impact; name operational constraints and alternative innovation choices.
- **Entrepreneurship and business venturing:** separate assumptions from validated demand; compare options against available market evidence and downside risk.
- **Marketing and analytics:** do not treat engagement correlation as causal sales impact; check sample, attribution, measurements and comparison period.
- **Consultancy:** start from the client's actual decision, then compare feasible alternatives, evidence, implementation risk and measurable outcomes; never invent client interviews or proprietary numbers.
- **Reflective work:** apply a relevant model to the student's genuine event; if the event is not documented, request it or provide generic reflection methods only.
- **Research methods:** make research question, constructs, sample, ethics, measures and limits compatible; never write as if proposed fieldwork occurred.

## Quality comparison (never a mark promise)

- **Description:** names a theory, source or case without explaining its relevance.
- **Application:** places evidence or theory against the specific case.
- **Analysis:** identifies a mechanism or relationship and its assumptions.
- **Evaluation:** tests at least one credible rival account or limitation and explains its significance.
- **Synthesis:** weighs multiple appropriately inspected sources and reaches a justified, appropriately qualified answer.

These are a diagnostic ladder only. The actual current marking rubric defines the assessment, not a made-up 70% threshold. Prefer one defensible judgement over elaborate but empty vocabulary.

## Worked synthetic example

Weak: "Digital marketing increases business performance." This has no defined outcome, business context, timeframe or credible countercase. A useful next question is: "Which performance measure, in which business setting, and what would distinguish a marketing effect from a seasonal rise?" The tutor does **not** invent data to supply the answer.

After the student's response, link the refined thesis to verified Claim IDs. If the evidence cannot support a causal claim, state the limit; do not simply make the sentence stronger.
